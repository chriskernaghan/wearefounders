EmailFig is an email marketing tool that doesn't gate features behind tiers. Automation, segmentation, signup forms, landing pages and the drag-and-drop editor are all available on the free plan, and what you buy when you pay is the removal of a small "Sent with EmailFig" line from the footer of your emails. For anyone who has spent an afternoon working out which Mailchimp tier unlocks the thing they actually needed, that's a refreshing way to lay it out.

The free plan is the headline. 4,000 subscribers and 10,000 emails a month with no card and no trial clock is well beyond what most competitors give away, and it's enough to run a real newsletter rather than evaluate one. Paid plans start at $6 a month, and there's a bring-your-own-Amazon-SES option that drops the cost further at volume by moving the sending onto your own account.

One quirk to understand before you upgrade. The $6 Orchard plan caps you at 500 subscribers and 5,000 emails, which is below what the free plan allows, so moving up from free costs you capacity unless you jump further up the ladder. The pricing page doesn't itemize the tiers above Orchard either, giving only a range, so working out what your actual list size costs takes more effort than it should.

It's upfront about what's missing. There's no SMS and no A/B testing of campaigns, so if split-testing subject lines is central to how you work, this isn't the tool. For a founder sending a straightforward newsletter or a lifecycle sequence, the economics are hard to argue with.
