Most translation tools hand you one answer and ask you to trust it. MachineTranslation.com runs your text through 22 leading AI models simultaneously, then compares the outputs to surface the version with the highest agreement between them. The reasoning is straightforward: where independent models converge on the same phrasing, that phrasing is more likely to be right, and where they diverge, that's exactly the sentence worth a human eye.

You can see all of it. Rather than a black-box verdict, the outputs sit side by side so you can compare wording, tone, and terminology across models before picking one, which is genuinely useful for marketing copy or anything where register matters as much as literal accuracy. Coverage runs to 330+ languages from a single interface, so there's no juggling separate subscriptions or switching tools per language pair.

It's built by Tomedes, a translation company with two decades behind it, and there's a public API plus an MCP connector if you'd rather call it from your own stack than use the web app. Worth noting what it isn't: the team are clear that certified or legally binding documents belong with human translators, and that specialised legal, medical, or technical content should get a professional review before publication. There's a free tier to try it, with paid plans for heavier use.
