xTiles is a visual workspace built around boards rather than documents, so notes, tasks, links, and files sit side by side instead of stacked in a page tree. The pitch is daily planning more than knowledge management, and the product is shaped accordingly, with a planner, recurring tasks, a Pomodoro timer, and calendar sync to Google and Outlook.

The part that makes it relevant right now is the MCP layer. Claude and ChatGPT connect directly to the workspace, and there are prebuilt workflows for a morning brief assembled from your calendar and inbox, an evening reflection, a weekly review, and task triage. That turns the board into something an assistant can read and write rather than a place you go to type.

The free tier is more generous than most in this category: unlimited pages and projects, three personal spaces, calendar integration, and 1,000 AI credits, with uploads capped at 5 MB and planner history at two weeks. Plus is $19 a month or around $149 a year and removes the caps. Pro at $35 monthly adds unlimited shared spaces and unlimited AI integrations.

The obvious question is why this rather than Notion, and the honest answer is the board-first layout and the planning focus. If you think in spatial arrangements rather than nested pages, it fits better. If you don't, Notion already does most of this.
