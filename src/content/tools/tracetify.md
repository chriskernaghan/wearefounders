Most competitor tools tell you where a rival stands today. Tracetify tries to answer a more useful question for anyone starting out: how did they get their first users? Paste a domain and it pulls from twelve public sources, the Wayback Machine, Hacker News and Reddit archives, backlink indexes, Google Trends, SERP data, ad libraries, and reconstructs a dated timeline of first mentions, quiet periods, directory waves and launch spikes, in about 45 seconds.

The thing that makes it credible is that you can check it before spending anything. Hundreds of completed reports sit public and free to read, Base44, ElevenLabs, Gamma, PhotoAI among them, with every line linking back to the dated source it came from. If a source fails or a site blocks the crawler, the report says so rather than filling the gap with a guess, and each verdict states its confidence and what evidence would change it.

Each report ends with three concrete moves drawn from the launch period rather than from current traffic, on the reasoning that what a company does at 500 visitors is copyable and what it does at 500,000 usually isn't. Beyond traces there's a set of research tools, keyword difficulty, backlinks, site audits, AI visibility, plus competitor monitoring and an MCP integration for Claude Code, Cursor and Codex. Reading existing reports is free with no card; credits start at $19.90 one-time or $9.90/month, and every tool shows its credit cost before you run it. It works best on products with a real public footprint, so very new or very private companies leave less to trace.
